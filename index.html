The method that actually works

Ask a hundred CW operators how they got fluent and you'll hear the same story: Morse code is a listening skill, not a memorization exercise. Fluent operators don't decode dits and dahs — they hear di-dah-di-dit and the letter L simply appears in their head, the way you hear a spoken word without spelling it. Everything on this site is built around getting you to that reflex, called Instant Character Recognition (ICR).

DO
Learn characters at 20 wpm or faster. At that speed you physically can't count elements — each character becomes one sound pattern. If you can still count at 20, go faster. Use the Farnsworth knob to slow the spacing (10 wpm effective is the classic starting point) so you get thinking time without ever hearing slow, stretched characters.
DO
Add characters gradually (Koch method). Start with just K and M at full speed, drill until you're at 90% accuracy, then add one character. You're always practicing real copying, never re-learning slow habits.
DO
Practice daily, in short sessions. The near-universal recommendation is 15–30 minutes every day, split in two if you can. Skipping days sets you back more than short sessions ever will. Stop when your focus drops — tired practice trains sloppiness.
DO
Send what you copy. Sending drills lock in rhythm and spacing, and hearing your own fist decoded is instant feedback. Copy a word, then key it back.
DO
Train on real material early. Callsigns, Q-codes, CW abbreviations, and QSO phrases are what you'll actually hear on the air — they're all in Copy practice.
DON'T
Don't use charts, code trees, or mnemonics to decode. Visual lookups and sound-alikes add a translation step that hard-caps your speed around 10 wpm and is painful to unlearn. The chart below is a reference for sending and curiosity — your ears do the learning.
DON'T
Don't learn at 5 wpm. Slow characters teach you to count. Speed up the characters, stretch the spacing instead.
DON'T
Don't wait until it's perfect. Missing a character? Let it go and catch the next one. Chasing a missed letter costs you the three after it. (And protect your hearing — lowest comfortable volume, especially with headphones.)

Your path on this site

STAGE 1

Hear the sounds

Browse the character groups and drill each group until single characters are instant. Letters first, then numbers, then punctuation & prosigns.

STAGE 2

Build the alphabet

Work the 41-lesson Koch course. 90% accuracy unlocks the next character automatically. This is the daily-15-minutes core.

STAGE 3

Copy real traffic

Words, callsigns, abbreviations, and QSO exchanges. Push effective speed up as head-copy gets comfortable.

STAGE 4

Work your fist

Straight key or iambic paddle with a live decoder. Send what you just copied and aim for clean spacing.

Goal benchmarks used by clubs and classes: solid copy at 13–15 wpm is conversational; 25 wpm send/receive is the CW Operators Club bar. From zero, most people who practice daily are making real QSOs in two to four months.
